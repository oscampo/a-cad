# 9. Graphical simulations

Moving the drawing over time: rotary and linear actuators, the simulation player, graphs of measurements, gears, rack and pinion, and relations that only hold while the elements touch.

## Actuators

**Actuators** (group after Fillet): **MR** (rotary) is defined by choosing the line and the end of it that will be the center of rotation (tap them in any order, or select them before choosing the tool; it turns counterclockwise by default, or clockwise, and the direction is shown by the circular arrow); **ML** (linear) with the tail and the head (tap the two points, or select them first: the first one is the tail). You give them a **displacement** or a **speed** with respect to time `t`, through an expression (`90*t`, `30*sin(2π*t)`) or a "time; value" table. Tap their label, or their name in the element tree (group "Actuators"), to edit or delete them; the selection filter can turn off "Actuators and graphs". **Simulation** opens the time bar: it plays and moves the drawing by solving the constraints (the MR line cannot be fixed horizontal or vertical, nor its length changed in the ML); the player has step back/forward, loop repeat (↻), back-and-forth (⇄) and speed from 0.5x to 2.0x; when you stop, the drawing returns to how it was.

## Graphs

**Graphs** (actuator group): tap where the lower left corner goes and type the relations "variable1 vs variable2", one per line (the first goes on the vertical axis): `MR1 vs t`, `MR1.v vs t`, `D1 vs t`, or two vectors of the same size, by name or with an expression (with `x:0..1;10` and `f(x):x.^2`: `f(x) vs x`, `x.^2 vs x`, `sin(x) vs x`). Variables: `t`, the actuators (`MR1`, `ML1`; their speed with `.v`, for example `ML1.v`), the named dimensions, the variables of the texts and the vectors. Each side accepts operations and functions with those variables: `p1.x/p1.y vs t`, `ML1.d-p1.x vs t`. The graph is read at its place on the page, in the **reading order** (from top to bottom and, within a row, from left to right, by its upper left corner), like a dimension with a formula: it sees the variables and functions defined **before** it, and what is defined or redefined further down does not affect it. The **x** belongs to each graph: if the drawing already defines `x` before the graph (a range or a vector), those values are used one by one; if not, the graph uses a generic vector of *Samples* values between the limits of its **x1** axis (from −10 to 10 if you do not set them), so `sin(x)/x vs x` is drawn without defining anything. What you see is what gets sampled: with `x'cm` and the axis from 0 to 900 mm, x goes from 0 to 90 cm. With no limits on **x1** and an x with units, the graph **focuses itself**: the window goes from 0 to the largest of the measurements with that magnitude that the relation reads, also inside its functions (with a beam of L = 900 mm, from 0 to 900 mm). If there are no such measurements (an x without units, or cuts written as numbers, `fsn(x, 0.5, 0)`), the window is the stretch where the curve does something (jumps, kinks or curvature): outside it, the curve is constant or straight. A curve that does something over the whole range (`sin(x)/x`, `x^2`) stays from −10 to 10. With no limits on y, the y window is that of what is seen in the x window. The time `t` is defined only by the simulation player (its "to"): the graph no longer has an end time. With the graph selected (a tap, or when dragging it), controls appear on its sides; unselected, it looks clean, only the graph: four elastic sliders, at the top (horizontal pan, ⇆), on the right (vertical pan, ⇅), at the bottom (horizontal zoom, ↔) and on the left (vertical zoom, ↕): while you hold them away from the center the window moves (to the right or upward, or the opposite) or zooms in (right or up) or out, faster the further you move them (the pan is slow near the center, for fine adjustment), and when you release them they return to the center; a double tap on a slider returns that axis to the **saved view**. To save it, leave the graph at the position and zoom you want and tap the round button in the corner, between the zoom sliders (it fills in and saves the limits of x1 and y1); afterwards you can move and zoom freely and the double tap recovers it. A double tap on that button deletes the saved view; without it, the double tap on a slider returns the axis to automatic limits. The **⤢ Fit** button in the upper right corner puts all the axes in automatic limits with one tap (the saved view does not change). Moving or zooming the window changes the axis limits (also in the graph's properties) and a generic x is recalculated within them. At the end of the line, `@y2`, `@x2` or `@x2 y2` draw it on the secondary axes (x1 bottom, x2 top, y1 left, y2 right). **Style of each relation**, in quotes at the end: color (`r g b c m y k w` or `#rrggbb`; `k` is the text color), line (`-` solid, `--` dashed, `:` dotted, `-.` dash-dot) and symbol (`o + * . x s d ^ v > < p h`: circle, plus, asterisk, point, cross, square, diamond, triangles, star and hexagon). `σ vs N "rx"` is a red cross with no line, `y vs x "k--d"` black dashed with diamonds and `Y vs X "o"` only circles: a **scatter plot**, to see how data are distributed (for example, from `randn`). With a symbol and no line, the line is not drawn; without quotes, a solid line in the color that comes next in order. In the graph's properties, below the relations, each one has its lists of color, line and symbol, which write that style. A relation of a single point without a symbol is marked with a circle. Tap a graph to select it (it is highlighted): drag it to move it and drag its edges or corners to resize it. A double tap opens its properties: title, relations, samples, position, size and, for each axis, its free name (for example `p12.x [cm]`, which serves as documentation because the axes do not show units), linear or logarithmic scale, minimum and maximum, and major and minor divisions. Points with "Trace point" draw their trajectory when the simulation runs and keep it when you stop it.

## Gear

**Gear** (Constraints group): select 2 lines, one rigid with each gear (for example the radius of each one that starts at the fixed center) and type the rotation ratio (negative if they turn in opposite directions; it accepts a formula, e.g. `-17/34`; with equal modules it is −z1/z2, or the ratio of the pitch or base diameters, not of the outside ones). First position the gears by hand, with the teeth not interfering, and then create the relation: the pose at that moment is the starting point and from then on the rotation of both is governed by the ratio. Drag any point of one of the gears and the other turns with the ratio, for several turns. Conditions: each gear turns around a fixed point (the end of the line that coincides with it) and its points are tied to the center, like those of a circular array. It does not model tooth contact. If you change `z` or `m`, position again and create the relation again.

## Rack

**Rack** (wheel and rack, Constraints group): select, in this order, the radial line of the wheel, the line of the rack (or the ground) and the point that advances, and type the ratio: how far the point advances for each radian the wheel turns (signed; the radius of the wheel. A wheel rolling to the right on the ground turns clockwise, so the ratio is −radius). For a wheel rolling without slipping, the point is its **center** (not a point on the rim: the app checks this and, if you chose the end of the wheel line at the rim, it uses the other end); for a pinion with its rack, a point of the rack. Drag the wheel or the point and the other moves with the ratio. The pinion must turn around a fixed point (or one tied to a fixed point with "Coincident"). The rack line gives the direction of advance. It does not model tooth contact.

## Finite relations

**Finite relations** ("Only within limits", in the properties of an element, in the list of its constraints): by default a tangency, a point on a line or on an arc holds even if the elements are not seen in contact (for the solver, lines and circles are infinite). With the box checked, the relation only holds while the elements **touch graphically**: their contact point falls within the limits (the segment between its ends, the sweep of the arc) and the separation is almost zero; outside that, the elements are left free. If they cross each other within a step, they are captured. With **Stop at edges** (it appears when you check "Only within limits") the element is not left free when it goes out through an edge: it stops there (the point at the end of the segment or of the arc; in a tangency, the end of the segment or of the arc rests on the other element) and the relation resumes when it goes back inside. It is useful for slots and contacts that begin and end: a Geneva drive is built with a finite "point on line" relation between the pin and each slot (the drive advances one slot per turn of the driver). With ideal geometry (a/d = sin(π/n)) the pin enters head-on through the mouth; with any other, it enters from the side and the drive makes a jump when capturing it. Available in: point on line, point on circle or arc, line-circle or arc tangent and tangent between arcs.

## Libraries

**Libraries**: a document with functions, scripts and tables that you want to use in others. In **Settings → Library** check "It is a library" and fill in its card: name, **alias** (a short prefix, e.g. `UF`), author, description and what is **public** (by default, its functions and scripts; what those need travels too, as private, and the examples do not travel). "Save to GitHub" leaves it in the `MyLibrary/` folder of the repository, with the extension `.alib.json` and a version that goes up by itself (v1, v2…); if it had been opened from another folder, it offers to send the original to the trash; "Open from GitHub" has the **Libraries** tab to reopen it. To use it in another document: **File → Insert library…**, choose it and confirm the alias. A block `▸ UtilidadesFatiga (UF) · v3` remains with a copy of its definitions (it works offline) and its names are used with the alias: `UF.C_(surf)(520'MPa, "machined")=`, `UF.Neuber_(steels)_(1,1)=`; the prefix is drawn in gray. It applies from its place on the page, like any definition, and its functions always use their own tables even if your document has others with the same name. If the library changes on GitHub, the block shows `⟳ v4`; in its properties (double tap), "Update" brings in the new version and first tells you which names are added or disappear. If the library uses another decimal separator, it is converted when inserted; if it uses another angle mode, the block warns you.
